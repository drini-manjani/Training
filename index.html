Rules that override the calendar

  1. Shins first. Any tibial pain that lasts past the warm-up: drop that week's plyos, keep the strength session, keep tibialis. Two flare-ups in a row: replace depth jumps with box jumps (step down) for the rest of the block.
  2. Never hard the day before a game. Friday is a primer on game weeks. If you played a midweek friendly or extra run, Friday is tibialis only.
  3. Intensity stays, volume flexes. Short on time or energy: cut sets, not kilos.
  4. Track two numbers only: the Wednesday (or Thursday) approach-jump chalk mark and the Tuesday squat top set. If the chalk mark drops 3 cm or more two weeks running, the next week is a deload at 70% whatever the table says.
  5. Tuesday shooting is fine after the strength session, not before it.
  6. Every barbell rep: 2–3 s controlled down, then drive up as fast as you possibly can. Never bounce out of the bottom. If you hit RIR 0, repeat that weight next time rather than adding.
  7. Landings are silent. Box jumps: always step down, never jump down. Depth jumps: step off the low box, land, explode onto the tall box, never rebound off the floor.

Bodyweight

94 kg at the start of the programme, 93.5 on 27 Aug, 92.5 on 4 Sep. Keep the cut at about 0.5 kg a week until ~89 kg (expected late October), then hold at maintenance calories for the rest of the season. The January–March strength block does not work in a deficit. Protein 1.8–2 g per kg throughout; carbs around training stay.

Why it is built this way

  • Strength plus plyometrics beats either alone. Every phase keeps one strength and one jump stimulus a week, never just one.
  • In basketball players, session frequency and programme length do not change the benefit of plyometrics, so low-frequency in-season plyos still work.
  • Strength is maintained for months with one session a week at 80–85% and a third of the volume, as long as the load stays heavy. One session every two weeks is not enough, which is why Albania gets a gym session if possible.
  • Plyometric jump gains are fully retained after two weeks of no jumping; long steady running is the one thing that erodes jump power, sprints do not. So trips are sprints only.
  • Depth jumps done box-to-box with a full-amortisation landing transfer best to countermovement jump height, and they are what your shins can tolerate.
